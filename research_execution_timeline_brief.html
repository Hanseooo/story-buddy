Training is not the first emergency.

The October technical-defense date and evidence requirement are not yet known. This brief plans against the conservative 1 October boundary, separates a final-results defense from a technical-progress defense, and protects the study from rushing into invalid training.

03

The actual critical path

GPU training is near the end. Every preceding step determines whether its output is scientifically usable.

Gate 1Approvals

Adviser, ethics, school, consent/assent, retention, adult participants.

Gate 2Corpus

Sanitized stories, two-person redaction review, frozen roles and styles.

Gate 3Images

Run the existing graph, hash exact assets, quarantine failures.

Gate 4Labels

Two blinded annotators, calibration, disagreements adjudicated.

Gate 5Freeze

Disjoint splits, immutable manifest, hashes and version pins.

Gate 6Train + test

Three seeds, validation-only selection, one held-out reading.

Why training now would be wrong

Without human-confirmed labels and a character-disjoint frozen manifest, the adapter can learn “a reference image was used” rather than “this is the same character.” Without version pins and immutable image bytes, the reported model cannot be reproduced. A quick run would consume time while producing evidence the paper should not use.

04

Two legitimate October branches

Use the branch the institution confirms. Do not quietly blend them.

Branch A · Final empirical defense

Completed findings are expected

Required minimum: approved participant procedures; final donated held-out corpus; dual labels and adjudication; frozen dataset; trained model; Objective-4 PRF1 with clustered uncertainty and label reliability; completed Tool B and Tool C sessions if Objectives 3 and 5 remain in the manuscript.

Feasibility: high risk. It remains plausible only if approval and participant access clear immediately and the team works exclusively on the research path.

Failure response: formally amend scope or move the defense. Do not relabel pilot evidence as final.

Branch B · Technical-progress defense

Verified implementation and pilot evidence are accepted

Required minimum: live end-to-end fixture demonstration; Tool A functional matrix; research-integrity tests; three-story paid smoke; annotation workflow demonstration with clearly marked pilot rows; frozen methodology and pre-registration; honest readiness and risk report.

Feasibility: realistic even if donated-story approval is delayed.

Claim boundary: demonstrates technical readiness and study feasibility only. It does not satisfy final Objective 3, 4, or 5 findings.

Conservative timeline to October

Dates are planning gates, not promises. Recalculate immediately when the exact defense date and approval status are known.

24–29 August

Scope and governance gate

  • Obtain the exact defense date and evidence requirement.
  • Confirm adviser decisions: study label, Tool B, Tool C, evaluator pools, Objective-4 corpus provenance, and required baselines.
  • Complete or submit Ethics Stage 1, adult-participant protocol, school approval, consent/assent, retention, withdrawal, and translation requirements.
  • Verify live application of research migrations and remove/quarantine pilot rows.
  • Output: a dated go/no-go record for Branch A versus Branch B.
30 August–6 September

Operational proof

  • Implement the minimal offline Tool A functional-verification script already specified.
  • Run one fixture through the complete research flow.
  • Run the smallest three-story paid smoke and correct operational failures only.
  • Pin current production model/prompt/config versions for corpus generation.
  • Output: verified pilot bundle, functional matrix, cost/latency trace, and readiness decision.
7–14 September

Generate controlled corpora

  • Only after governance permits: freeze donated candidate roles and styles before outcomes are observed.
  • Generate synthetic train/validation stories and approved donated test candidates through the existing production graph.
  • Quarantine roster, asset, or terminal pipeline failures under the recorded replacement rule.
  • Materialize exact blinded reference/scene pairs and verify hashes.
  • Output: completed StoryMemory bundles, asset inventory, and blinded pair queue.
15–21 September

Human reference labels

  • Calibrate two ordinary annotators on pilot items that cannot enter the frozen study dataset.
  • Run independent blinded annotation; monitor missing images and fatigue without exposing agreement.
  • Adjudicate every disagreement across the complete label fields.
  • Reconcile pair statuses from annotation truth and clear pilot contamination.
  • Output: complete human labels, agreement statistics, adjudication rate, and deviation log.
22–28 September

Freeze and train

  • Freeze character-disjoint train/validation/test splits and immutable asset bytes.
  • Record dataset hash, model revision, LLaMA-Factory version, prompt version, seeds, and image limits.
  • Train seeds 0, 1, and 2; choose checkpoints using validation only.
  • Debug only against train/validation before opening the held-out test.
  • Output: three adapter artifacts, validation table, frozen analysis inputs.
29 September–5 October

One-time evaluation gate

  • Open the held-out donated test once after all choices are frozen.
  • Compute precision, recall, F1, character-clustered confidence intervals, malformed-output counts, and required paired controls.
  • Apply the pre-registered deployment ladder separately from the reported Objective-4 result.
  • Do not deploy the adapter merely to improve the defense narrative.
  • Output: immutable Objective-4 results table and interpretation.
October · before defense

Human evaluation and defense package

  • Administer the approved Tool B and Tool C only under the confirmed adult-participant protocol.
  • Code Tool B meaning units, double-code the predeclared subset, and retain disagreement.
  • Create the mixed-methods joint display without a composite score.
  • Rehearse evidence, limitations, non-claims, live fallback, and exact status.
  • Output: defense deck, reproducibility appendix, limitations slide, and live demo backup.
If approvals are not clear by early September

Branch A becomes increasingly unlikely. Freeze further paid/full-corpus work and prepare Branch B unless the adviser formally approves a changed corpus or schedule. This is a planning judgment, not an institutional deadline.

07

What to keep, amend, and stop

Scope control protects the research; it is not a reduction in rigor.

Keep

Evidence that carries the objectives

  • Ethics and participant protection
  • Character-disjoint frozen dataset
  • Two annotators plus adjudication
  • Precision, recall, F1 and clustered uncertainty
  • Human-label reliability
  • Approved Tool B and Tool C if final
  • Reproducibility pins and deviation log
Amend before labels

Only with adviser approval

  • Corpus provenance or donated-only test rule
  • Required baseline/control set
  • DreamBench++ transfer
  • Data-scaling ablation
  • Downstream post-swap expert comparison
  • Study-design label and integration procedure
  • Any Objective 3 numeric redesign
Stop now

Work the defense does not need

  • New product features
  • Further artifact styling after print verification
  • Modal/vLLM deployment before results
  • Dashboards for offline analysis
  • Serving the LoRA merely because it exists
  • Unrequested pipeline refactors
  • Optional export unless formally retained
Frozen does not mean impossible to change.

It means changes must be dated, justified, approved, and made before observing the affected labels/results. CLIP, DINOv2, zero-shot, and prompted controls are described as non-negotiable in the frozen pre-registration even though other documents call comparisons optional. Reconcile that contradiction formally; do not silently choose the easier wording.

What to bring to the technical defense

Package evidence so the panel can distinguish implementation, pilot evidence, and final findings at a glance.

System

Technical evidence

  • Architecture and locked ADR map
  • Moderation and privacy ordering
  • Functional-verification matrix
  • Deterministic test summary
  • Live or recorded happy-path demo
  • Failure/offline fallback
Research

Method evidence

  • Five objectives and instrument map
  • QUAN-dominant mixed-methods rationale
  • Approved Tool B and Tool C versions
  • Pre-registration and amendments
  • Corpus provenance and split diagram
  • Non-circularity explanation
Results

Claim evidence

  • Clearly labelled pilot versus final tables
  • PRF1, uncertainty and IRR
  • Expert themes with short evidence excerpts
  • ISO characteristic results
  • Mixed-methods joint display
  • Limitations and non-claims slide

One-sentence defense posture

If final evidence is complete

“We developed a safety-gated picture-book pipeline and evaluated it through independent expert interpretation, human-referenced judge classification, and perceived software quality; each result is reported only for the sample and construct its instrument supports.”

If October is a progress defense

“The implementation and research apparatus are verified at pilot scale; participant-dependent findings remain pending prior approvals, and we do not present fixture or synthetic results as final capstone outcomes.”
