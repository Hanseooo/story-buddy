My honest thoughts on this capstone

The documentation has outrun the project, and that's now your biggest risk. You have ~4,700 lines of genuinely excellent docs sitting on ~370 lines of backend code and an unrun $2 probe. Every document says, correctly, that it is contingent on Phase 0.5 — which means the highest-information hour available to you this month is running consistency and scoring 54 images, and it has been available for weeks. There's a failure mode where refining the plan becomes a comfortable substitute for exposing it to reality. You're past the point of diminishing returns on planning; further doc polish is procrastination with good posture. Run the probe this week.

Only one thing on your critical path can't be bought back with effort: ethics. Code can be crashed, rating sessions can be compressed, the fine-tune is literally a weekend. Ethics Stage 1 is months and starts when you submit. If you do exactly one thing after the probe, it's that submission — now carrying the withdrawal clause and adult-rater protocol so you don't file twice.

Own these documents personally before the proposal defense. This is the advice I'd most want you to hear. The docs are written in a very sophisticated voice, and a panel examines you, not the PDF. If a panelist asks "why McNemar's test?" or "why is the bootstrap clustered by character?" and the answer isn't in your head, the sophistication inverts into a liability — it signals the document knows things you don't. Pick the five hardest arguments (non-circularity, the claim ladder, why the OFF arm isn't a strawman, why beating your own base isn't impressive, the caption fix) and rehearse re-deriving each out loud, from nothing, until you can. A mock defense with your three researchers as a hostile panel is one evening and worth more than any further edit.

Check your genre against your school. The docs target IMRaD/IEEE and pre-registration culture — which is the right science. But many Philippine capstone panels grade against a Chapter 1–5 template, expect ISO 25010 prominently, and weight a flawless live demo over methodological rigor. Confirm the required manuscript format with your adviser early; if it's Chapter 1–5, the mapping in research_direction_and_goals.md §9 needs a second column. And treat the demo as a deliverable in its own right: keep-warm the worker, pre-generate a fallback book, rehearse the failure path. Panels forgive a modest result; they don't forgive a frozen progress bar.

Budget for corpus recruitment being slower than the plan implies. Fifty to seventy donated stories means a teacher giving class time, consent forms going home, and coming back — return rates of 30–50% are normal. Ask two or three classes, not one, and start the principal conversation the same week ethics is filed, not after clearance.

Put a date on the de-scope ladder. It's currently a good list with no trigger. Write "on [date], whatever isn't done gets cut in ladder order" into the roadmap and have your adviser hold you to it — at 2 a.m. in month four, a pre-committed date decides better than you will. And remember rung C and the offline-only judge are honorable outcomes the docs already defend; the project's spine is RQ2+RQ5 on the prompted judge, and that spine alone is a strong capstone.

The short version: this is a well-designed, defensible, genuinely interesting project whose plan quality is in the top percentile of capstones — and whose next unit of progress is empirical, not editorial. Go spend the two dollars.
